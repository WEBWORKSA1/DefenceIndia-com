iDEX for Founders — How Start-ups Win Defence Contracts Without a Lobbyist

By DefenceIndia Desk · 2026-08-22 · 1 min read · Explainer
Advertisement

Innovations for Defence Excellence (iDEX), run by the Defence Innovation Organisation under the Department of Defence Production, is the fastest route for a start-up or MSME to get a paid development contract from the armed forces.

How it works

  1. The services publish problem statements through Defence India Start-up Challenges (DISC) — 12 editions so far — and the higher-value ADITI (Acing Development of Innovative Technologies) scheme.
  2. Applicants submit a technical and commercial proposal on idex.gov.in.
  3. Shortlisted teams pitch to a committee including the user service.
  4. Winners sign a Simplified Procurement Procedure agreement — a fixed-milestone development contract.
  5. Successful prototypes move to procurement under the DAP 2020 iDEX category, which allows an order without a fresh tender.

The money

  • DISC: grants up to ₹1.5 crore per project, matched by the founder's own investment (grant covers up to 90 percent for early stages).
  • ADITI: up to ₹25 crore for deep-tech projects — hypersonics, quantum, underwater domain, space.
  • Over 400 start-ups and MSMEs contracted, with procurement orders exceeding ₹2,000 crore in aggregate.

What the services actually want (recent DISC themes)

Loitering munitions, counter-drone, AI-based target recognition, secure communications, high-altitude batteries, lightweight ballistic protection, underwater sensors, satellite imagery analytics, additive manufacturing of spares, and simulators.

Mistakes first-timers make

  • Pitching a product instead of answering the specific problem statement.
  • Underestimating certification (CEMILAC, DGQA, MIL-STD environmental testing) in time and cost.
  • Ignoring the field trial calendar — Ladakh winter trials happen once a year; miss it and you lose 12 months.
  • No plan for the "valley of death" between prototype acceptance and a production order — budget for 18 to 24 months.

Supporting ecosystem

The Technology Development Fund (DRDO, up to ₹50 crore), SRIJAN import-substitution portal listing 30,000-plus items, defence corridors in UP and Tamil Nadu, and DPSU vendor registration (HAL, BEL, BDL each run online portals) round out the pathway.
